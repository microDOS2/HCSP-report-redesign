Part III · Funding that gives up no ownership

The funding layer: $37.33B of known pools, three doors in

Climate-era money is contracting; money for PFAS "forever chemicals" is at its all-time peak. Ninety-two programs screened (dead ones kept on record), fifteen pursuits ranked by expected value, and a 12-month deadline wall across the pools that survived — with rolling federal cycles through 2027.

§8 · Funding landscape — Part III

Climate-era money is contracting; PFAS money is at its all-time peak

The addressable grant universe for a wastewater-destruction technology vendor is smaller than it was in 2024 — but better targeted. The programs that died were ones HCSP was unlikely to win anyway; the money that survived concentrates in PFAS destruction, water reuse, and defense resilience — precisely HCSP's value proposition. The strategic error to avoid is writing proposals to dead programs; the strategic opportunity is that PFAS funding is counter-cyclical.

Four structural events define the year. First, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act (OBBBA, P.L. 119-21, July 4, 2025) rescinded unobligated IRA climate-justice appropriations, and EPA terminated 781 environmental justice grants and closed the Office of Environmental Justice entirely — dead as funding sources, and never a fit for a for-profit technology company anyway. Second, the Department of Energy's demonstration arm is gone: the Office of Clean Energy Demonstrations was abolished in the November 2025 reorganization after terminating 24 awards (~$3.7B) in May 2025 and roughly $7.56B more across DOE offices in October 2025. There is no successor office and no reason to plan around one.

Third, two programs were interrupted and restored. FEMA's BRIC program — cancelled in April 2025 — was court-restored, and a combined FY2024–25 NOFO for $1B closed July 23, 2026; BRIC funds states and localities, so HCSP is at most an indirect vendor partner. More consequentially, SBIR/STTR lapsed 6.5 months and were reauthorized on April 13, 2026 through September 30, 2031, with higher ceilings and a new "Strategic Breakthrough" Phase II worth up to $30M at large agencies — the most reliable direct-apply channel for a company of HCSP's profile is fully restarted. Fourth, the BIL's PFAS-dedicated water money is in its final appropriation year: the last $1B of the $5B EC-SDC program (May 2026), a reaffirmed $4B of DWSRF PFAS money, and the closing $1B CWSRF Emerging Contaminants supplement — which covers wastewater, stormwater, and landfill leachate.

The counterweight — and the thesis of this layer — is that PFAS-specific money is expanding even as general climate money contracts. The 3M public-water-system settlement alone has paid out more than $3.5B to Phase 1 claimants, with installments running through 2033–2036; Tyco ($750M) and BASF ($316.5M) payouts began in March–April 2026. New Jersey's court-approved PFAS Abatement Fund (August 7, 2026) stands up a $795M state vehicle with at least $150M routed to sewage treatment plants. Wisconsin's Acts 200/201 release more than $130M, including direct grants to landfills for on-site leachate treatment. The market signal is unambiguous: destruction technology is moving from pilot novelty to funded line item. HCSP's task is to be specified, not just eligible.

§9 · The grant register

Ninety-two programs, screened — and three doors into them

Roughly 90% of the dollar volume relevant to HCSP flows to utilities, municipalities, and public systems — not to technology vendors. Winning this money is therefore primarily a business-development exercise (getting HCSP written into other entities' applications), not a grant-writing exercise.

Pursuit resources should be organized around three access modes, in descending order of dollar potential. Mode (b) — named technology in a partner's application — is where the big money is, and the template transaction already exists: the City of Orlando secured a federal BIL grant through Florida's CWSRF (~$800K, February 2024) to fund a demonstration of supercritical water oxidation (SCWO) — a way of destroying waste with very hot, high-pressure water — by 374Water at its Iron Bridge facility; that demonstration converted into a five-year waste-destruction-services license (March 2026) and then a $3M–$5M full-scale PFAS destruction hub (June 2026). The pattern repeats at OC San (6-ton/day SCWO through the standing RISE program) and in Minnesota (an ENRTF-funded SCWO biosolids project, December 2025). The lesson: the grant proposal is written by the utility; the vendor's job is to supply the engineering package, cost data, and performance evidence that make the application winnable.

Mode (a) — direct-apply programs — are fewer but actionable now: Reclamation's DWPR, the ONR Long-Range BAA, SERDP/ESTCP, all SBIR/STTR vehicles, NYSERDA's waste-to-fuels solicitation, PA PENNVEST (unusually, private owner/operators are eligible), AZ WIFA augmentation project teams, and USDA B&I loan guarantees. Mode (c) — vendor/subrecipient to prime contractors — monetizes demand HCSP cannot touch directly: DoD's firefighting-foam (AFFF) stockpile disposal is executed through installation-level contracts with prime environmental contractors, and DoD's 2024–25 demonstration series concluded on-site destruction beats off-site disposal. Allocate roughly 40% of pursuit effort to Mode (b), 35% to Mode (a), 25% to Mode (c): the direct applications generate credibility; the partnerships generate dollars.

International & NGO channels — G-081 through G-092

Twelve non-US-federal channels round out the register, and they split honestly into three kinds of access. Direct doors — ADB's Technology Innovation Challenge (a true $450K grant a for-profit can apply for itself, Pacific islands included), AB InBev's 100+ Accelerator ($100K pilots with beverage majors, built for the distillery/brewery industry), the GEF Challenge Program for Adaptation Innovation (the one GEF window inviting private-sector innovators directly), IDB Lab, the Water-AI Nexus at Penn, and WEF/WRF LIFT vetting (free, but non-cash). Via-host doors — GCF's Simplified Approval Process (up to $25M per project, but only through an Accredited Entity), EREF research grants (academic PI required; HCSP is the industry partner and site host), Caribbean Development Bank (supplier/EPC buying process on sovereign water projects), and Rotary Global Grants (club-hosted; HCSP can only be the equipment supplier). Not grants at all — WaterEquity's Water & Climate Resilience Fund is $2–15M of equity/mezzanine patient capital (money that buys ownership), and Breakthrough Energy Discovery mixes small earliest-stage (TRL 0–2) grants with an ownership-taking SAFE behind a ≥500 Mt CO2e/yr screen HCSP may not pass. None of these replace the Mode (b) utility-partner route for dollars; they add islands, data-center, and beverage-industry surface area the federal register does not reach.

Closed doors — the candid no-go list

Killing pursuits is as valuable as starting them. The following receive zero proposal effort, regardless of surface appeal:

  • USDA REAP grants — paused. No FY2026 NOFO; no grant awards until regulations are rewritten under EO 14315, and prior applicants without executed agreements must reapply. (Guaranteed loans remain open.)
  • EPA environmental justice programs — dead. 781 grants terminated, offices closed, unobligated funds rescinded by OBBBA; litigation is ongoing but these programs targeted community nonprofits, not technology vendors.
  • DOE OCED — abolished. No successor office, ~$11.3B in awards terminated, FY2026 request was $0; do not build a plan on revival.
  • 48C Advanced Energy Project Credit — closed. All $10B allocated across two rounds; OBBBA capped the program with no Round 3 expected.
  • Brewers Association / Distilled Spirits Council — no programs. Neither runs sustainability grants or cost-share pilots; distillery/brewery industries must be funded by customer-paid pilots or state programs (e.g., CEC FPIP for food processors).
  • XPRIZE Water Scarcity ($119M) — closed to new entrants. Registration is shut, 37 semifinalists were named in May 2026, and the scope is seawater desalination, not wastewater destruction. Monitor future Mohamed bin Zayed Water Initiative competitions only.
  • India distillery ZLD finance — inaccessible to a US vendor. The interest-subvention scheme (~6% p.a. for five years) funds Indian distilleries' CPCB-certified zero-liquid-discharge capital, not foreign equipment makers; the only route is an Indian JV or EPC partnership, with Imagine H2O Asia as the regional on-ramp.
  • CalRecycle Organics Grant Program — excludes the core feedstock. Biosolids are explicitly ineligible; only landfill-diverted food/green waste qualifies, so the program cannot fund an HCSP biosolids project even though for-profits may apply.
  • Also-rans to deprioritize: DARPA (water program concluded; no current water/PFAS topics), DHS S&T SBIR (no water topics; one cycle per year), EPA STAR (for-profits ineligible as prime), and CA cannabis wastewater funding (no program exists).

The common thread: every dead program is one where either the applicant type was wrong, the feedstock was wrong, or the politics moved. The pipeline should be re-screened against these three filters quarterly — the 2026–2027 environment is volatile enough that §8's list of the living and the dead will need a refresh by mid-2027.

§10 · The Top-15 pursuits

Fifteen pursuits, ranked by expected value — rolling federal cycles through 2027

Ranked by expected value (award size × probability × strategic leverage), the portfolio balances three near-term direct applications, five partner-mediated mega-pools, and the two best private programs. Everything not on this list is either dead, closed, or low-yield.

Two readings of the board matter more than any single row. First, the distribution of access modes confirms the §9 thesis: only eight of fifteen are direct-apply, and all five of the largest pools (WI, NJ, TX, EC-SRF, WIFIA at scale) are partner-mediated — HCSP's most valuable grant asset is a portfolio of host utilities and landfills, not a proposal-writing team. Second, notice the barbell: rows 1–4 are small-dollar, high-probability credibility plays (sub-$1M federal R&D), while rows 6, 9, 10, and 12 are large-dollar, long-cycle infrastructure plays. There is almost nothing worth pursuing in the middle, which is why effort should be sequenced rather than spread evenly. The outliers to watch are WI Act 200/201 (unusually direct for a state program, because private landfills themselves are grantees) and PENNVEST (the only major state water-finance program where a private owner/operator applies directly) — both reward an owner-operator project structure.

Stacking rules

  • Rule 2 · Stack vertically, never horizontally. The correct stack is sequential across project phases and non-overlapping across cost categories: a DWPR pilot ($800K federal) funds technology validation → a state grant or cost-share (WI Act 201, NJ Water Bank, CEC EPIC matching funds) funds the first commercial deployment → WIFIA debt (49% of eligible costs) finances scale-out at the utility host, with USDA B&I guarantees covering HCSP's own manufacturing expansion. Federal programs generally prohibit using one federal grant as the non-federal cost share for another on the same scope; cost share must come from state money, settlement money, customer payments, or private capital. Assume every application is audited on this point.
  • Rule 3 · One anchor utility per state program. SRF and EC funds flow through state priority lists; two HCSP-sponsored applications competing in the same state's Intended Use Plan dilute both. Pick one anchor host per state (a sewage treatment plant with a biosolids/PFAS cost crisis, a landfill with ELG exposure, or a small water system with settlement cash), put HCSP in their application as the named technology, and replicate the Orlando conversion path: funded demonstration → service license → hub.

Private & catalytic capital, disciplined. The private money that truly gives up no ownership is real but small: Imagine H2O's zero-equity accelerator and recoverable Pilot Fund, PRIME Coalition's recoverable grants for first-of-a-kind hardware, WRF's 1:1 Tailored Collaboration match (≤$150K), and Elemental Impact's DCII ($500K–$5M per project through 2027). Burnt Island, Microsoft CIF, Breakthrough Energy, and S2G are equity — not grants — and route to the capital-raise track. Hyperscaler water-replenishment programs are offtake revenue, not grants. Discipline about this distinction saves months of misdirected effort.

The words we use — a plain-English glossary
SCWO
Supercritical water oxidation — a way of destroying waste with very hot, high-pressure water.
PFAS
"Forever chemicals" — man-made chemicals that barely break down in nature.
COD
Chemical oxygen demand — a measure of how much organic waste is in the water.
AFFF
Firefighting foam — a major source of PFAS pollution.
Autothermal
The system makes its own heat from the waste it destroys.
CapEx / OpEx
Purchase cost / running cost.
TAM / SAM / SOM
Total market / reachable market / what we could win.
TRL
Technology readiness level — how proven the technology is, on a 1–9 scale.
WDS
Waste destruction service — we run the destruction for you.
ZLD
Zero liquid discharge — a rule that no liquid waste may leave a site.
